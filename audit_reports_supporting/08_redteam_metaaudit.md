# Meta-Audit: Red-Team Review of Seven Independent Velantrim Audits

Method note: I re-read all seven reports in full and read the complete 1005-line source export end-to-end (not just the passages cited), then went back to verify roughly 20 specific quotes/paraphrases against the source directly. Findings below reflect that direct verification, not trust in the auditors' citations.

## 1. Per-report disconfirmation results

### 01 — Relation Semantics
- **Survives essentially intact**: Finding 1 (untyped map arrows reproduce the "связано" problem the prose warns against — verified verbatim against lines 13-14 and 254-269); Finding 4 (contested/conflicting folded into a flat acquisition-path list at lines 271-280 — verified word-for-word; this is a clean, low-cost, genuinely orthogonal-axis catch); Finding 7 (the sharpest relation-discipline tools — SOURCE≠REPRESENTATION≠CLAIM≠EVIDENCE≠BELIEF≠KNOWLEDGE, RANK≠AUTHORITY — sit in the legacy layer explicitly marked non-binding at line 840, while the front-layer "ОБРАТНОЕ ВЛИЯНИЕ" material reinvents looser prose — verified, and the self-referential point that this is the document's *own* "revision must not silently lose ground" principle being violated by its own layering is sharp and correct).
- **Weakened**: Finding 2 (ОБРАТНОЕ ВЛИЯНИЕ collapsing causal/control/constitutive verbs) is real but the "определяют" vs. "влияет" contrast with lines 210-221 is less of a hard contradiction than claimed — line 81 ("Связи определяют, что память сможет поднять по ассоциации") is a one-clause summary in a "short version" recap (line 85: "Коротко:"), and compressed recaps using stronger verbs than the detailed treatment is a stylistic looseness, not necessarily an incompatible epistemic claim. Finding 6 (chained-relations non-transitivity oversteps into formal math/legal domains) is logically valid but low-stakes: the document is explicitly about empirical/cognitive reasoning under uncertainty; no passage anywhere applies the transitivity warning to mathematical equality or delegation chains, so this is a hypothetical misapplication the document invites only if read with deliberate uncharity. ## 2. Cross-agent duplicate-root-cause clusters

This is the centerpiece. I found four genuine clusters (one matching the calibration example almost exactly), plus one pervasive stylistic pattern that is not itself a single root cause but is worth naming separately.

### Cluster 1 — Silent candidate/option attrition later functions as evidence against the option
**Root cause, in my own words**: The document has a real, well-built discipline for *settled-record* compression not silently gaining epistemic strength (line 742: "COMPRESSION ALONE MUST NOT SILENTLY INCREASE EPISTEMIC STRENGTH"). But nowhere does that discipline get extended to the sibling case of a *live candidate* — a hypothesis, an alternative explanation, a user preference, an unfamiliar category — quietly dropping out of active attention for a non-evidential reason (resource limits, staleness, personalization narrowing, attention narrowing), after which its absence, or the absence of disconfirming signal about it, functions downstream as if it had been ruled out on the merits. The "not active ≠ false" guardrail (line 759-760) covers the *status* confusion; nothing covers the *evidentiary-weight* version of the same worry.

**Instantiated by**: 02 (Finding 6 — compression test scoped to settled records only; Finding 9 — explicitly names this as the same failure reinvented in two places); 03 (Finding 6 — the "three mysteries" at lines 113/408/730 are one problem in three costumes); 04 (Finding 2 — personalization loop unblocked; Finding 3 — identical gap at the background-memory edge, with the one candidate fix, offline consolidation, parked as an unplaced horizon at line 787); 05 (Finding 3 — Essence-driven personalization feedback is compression-laundering the document half-names but never connects to §738-746's own tools).

**Verification**: I independently confirmed all the cited passages (lines 113, 210-229, 408, 730, 741-743, 787, 799) exist as quoted, sit in five separately-drafted sections of the source (memory-edge, understanding-edge, semantic-clarification, person-understanding boundary, sufficiency section), and genuinely share the same shape. This is not four auditors agreeing on a vibe — it is the same lexical/structural pattern recurring across independently-written parts of the *source document itself*, which is exactly the standard that should justify treating a cluster as real (see §3, AI-consensus-as-evidence, below).

**Judgment**: Holds up, and holds up *more* strongly for having been found from four different edges of the map using four different vocabularies (pruning-laundering, blind-spot-aperture, self-confirming-loop, compression-laundering) — a genuine architectural gap, not four gaps. It is one fix: state, once, at the semantic-clarification level, that deprioritization/non-selection of a live candidate carries zero evidentiary weight against that candidate, and that this is the *same* rule as "compression must not silently increase epistemic strength," just applied to the candidate-space edge rather than the settled-record edge. Report 07's Finding 8 sits adjacent to this cluster (same passage, line 730) but is not a duplicate — it correctly identifies a *second*, independent harm (choice-foreclosure/agency, present even if the model stays perfectly accurate) that a fix for Cluster 1 would not itself resolve. Both should be kept, clearly labeled as siblings rather than collapsed into one.

### Cluster 2 — "Materially important" is the document's universal gating word and is never given operational content anywhere it's used
**Root cause, in my own words**: The document leans on "materially"/"materiality" as the load-bearing test for at least four distinct gate decisions — whether to keep a Trace back to grounds (line 490), whether the candidate space is sufficient or needs deepening (line 796), whether a deviation from a compressed routine baseline deserves attention (line 720-723), and whether a STOP is reopen-worthy (line 761, feeding off legacy §6's signal list at line 865). In every one of these four places the criterion offered is either purely intensional ("would change the conclusion" — which requires doing the investigation to know) or a bare synonym ("noticeably," "materially") applied to the very thing it's supposed to explain. The one place an actual content-list exists (legacy §6's materiality signals) is (a) marked non-binding reference material and (b) never stated as jointly-necessary vs. individually-sufficient, so even where it's consulted it doesn't resolve anything.

**Instantiated by**: 02 (Finding 5 — candidate-space sufficiency collapses into "convenient to stop," explicitly admitted by the document itself); 04 (Finding 6 — "material deviation" from a routine baseline has no criterion and isn't even its own open question); 05 (Finding 1 — the Trace/reopen "materially important" trigger has real content only in the non-binding legacy layer, plus a bootstrapping problem); 06 (Finding 6 — STOP/reopen materiality is never grounded at point of use, and "novelty" as a signal is trivially satisfied by any new candidate).

**Verification**: I confirmed all four textual anchors (490, 796, 720-723, 761/865) independently in the source; this is literally the same word doing the same undefined gating work in four separately-authored sections.

**Judgment**: This is a real, single root cause and arguably the most consequential one in the whole audit, because it is the term the document uses to decide *when to stop looking* — the single highest-leverage decision point in the architecture. Collapsing four "materiality is unspecified" findings into one does not make the problem smaller; if anything it raises the priority, because it shows the gap is not local to one edge (Trace, sufficiency, routine, STOP) but is a property of the term itself wherever the document reaches for it. The appropriate fix is not a numeric threshold (which the document explicitly and correctly refuses, line 313) but a single added sentence bridging the four sites to the one existing signal-list (legacy §6) plus a statement of whether signals are individually sufficient or must jointly hold — exactly the "no new primitive, bind an existing relation" style of fix 05 and 06 both independently proposed.

### Cluster 3 — Gradual/cumulative change is invisible to locally-scoped revision triggers, and the document never separates *why* a belief changed
**Root cause, in my own words**: Two related but distinguishable problems recur together: (a) if a revision trigger fires only on locally-material single steps, a 100→99.9→99.8→... drift is *by construction* invisible to it, since each step is defined as locally insignificant (this is Cluster 2's materiality gap, specifically applied to gradual change); and (b) even when a mismatch or drift is noticed, the document never disambiguates whether the *world's target state* moved, the system merely re-derived the same fixed evidence and got a different answer (zero new observation), or genuinely new evidence trickled in about an unchanged world — three cases requiring entirely different remedies (re-observe the world / audit the reasoner's stability / just update the arithmetic), fused under one "time/currentness" field everywhere it appears.

**Instantiated by**: 05 (Finding 6 — cumulative drift is a structural counterexample to local-materiality gating); 06 (Finding 2a — the divergence-diagnosis list never poses "did anything new get observed at all?" as its first-order question, illustrated with a clean math-retraction case with zero world/evidence component; Finding 2b — world-time vs. system/belief-time never separated, recurring independently in the memory/provenance section *and* the personalization section; Finding 3 — Self-Effect Discrimination A-D presupposes a new observation exists and structurally cannot reach the zero-new-observation case; Finding 4 — the cumulative-drift metaphor is neutral among world-drift/belief-drift/evidence-drift and the document's own proposed detection candidate only suits one of the three).

**Verification**: confirmed lines 528-540, 747-757, 628-637, 869-873, 705-712 all as quoted.

**Judgment**: Holds up as a real, sharply-drawn architectural gap — arguably the single sharpest individual finding in the entire corpus is 06's world-time/belief-time split, because it is (1) never stated anywhere the document uses "time," (2) demonstrably load-bearing (the pathology-report scenario shows the three readings genuinely license opposite actions), and (3) independently recurs in two unconnected, separately-drafted sections. Report 05's Finding 6 is a genuine independent discovery of the *materiality* half of this same underlying territory rather than a restatement of 06 — worth presenting as two components of one cluster rather than fully merging, since 05 approaches it from "the retention trigger structurally can't fire" and 06 approaches it from "the diagnosis, once triggered, is ambiguous about cause" — sequential stages of the same underlying blind spot.

### Cluster 4 — Essence's own scope-relativity discipline is stated once, generally, and not propagated to its siblings
**Root cause, in my own words**: The document explicitly re-scopes Essence, after adversarial review, as a bounded projection relative to question/context/scope, not a universally-safe compression (line 739), and states the general principle that the same source can yield different valid bounded views under different questions (line 857, VIEW A ≠ VIEW B). Two sibling constructs that are functionally the same kind of thing — a standing, reused, compact projection — never receive the same discipline: routine/baseline compression (explicitly meant for cross-time, cross-question reuse by its very purpose) and the user/person model (revised only along a *temporal* axis — past pattern vs. current identity — never along a *question/domain* axis).

**Instantiated by**: 05 (Finding 2 — routine/baseline is structurally cross-scope and never re-validated against a new question's scope); 07 (Finding 3 — user model doesn't inherit VIEW A ≠ VIEW B; the terse-weekday-user vs. major-life-decision-weekend counterexample is a clean, concrete demonstration).

**Verification**: confirmed lines 739, 857, 705-724 as quoted in both reports.

**Judgment**: Holds up as a real, minor-but-genuine cluster — smaller than Clusters 1-3 (two reports, two instances) but textually clean and non-redundant with the others: this is about *scope* portability specifically, not about candidates dropping out (Cluster 1) or materiality (Cluster 2) or time (Cluster 3). One fix serves both instances: state that "bounded projection relative to question/context/scope" is a general property of any standing compact representation the architecture builds (Essence, routine baseline, user model), not an Essence-specific rule.

### A pervasive pattern that is *not* itself a fifth cluster: "A≠B guardrails rarely pair with a positive B"
This surfaces, in some form, in nearly every report — 01 (Finding 3, the general statement of it, with the analogy/compression passages as the document's own counter-examples of doing it right); 02 (Finding 2, empty CANDIDATE SELECTION step; Finding 3, unspecified pairwise support/exclusivity); 04 (Finding 5, guardrail-as-value-not-check); 05 (Finding 4, near-universal flagging with no discharge criterion); 06 (Finding 6, materiality signals never bound to point of use); 07 (Finding 4, "показать последствия ≠ закрыть вариант" says what's forbidden, not what distinguishes influence from coercion). I am deliberately **not** folding this into Clusters 1-4, because unlike those, it is not one localized mechanism failing in multiple places — it is a general authorial habit (documented, and self-diagnosed by the document's own two counterexamples: ANALOGY→CANDIDATE UNDERSTANDING, and the COMPRESSION≠AGGREGATION≠INFERENCE≠DERIVATION≠CORROBORATION five-way split) that happens to be the *proximate cause* of several of the more specific gaps above. Treating it as a fifth "root cause" would overclaim: it's better described as a stylistic tendency that makes gaps like Clusters 1, 2, and 4 easier to leave unfixed, not a mechanism in its own right with its own fix.

## 3. Meta-audit findings

**Same findings under different names (beyond §2)**: I did not find additional undetected duplicates beyond the four clusters above. 05's Finding 2 (routine/baseline) and 07's Finding 3 (user model) look, on a skim, like they could be the same finding restated — they are not; they instantiate the same *root cause* (Cluster 4) but target genuinely different passages and were correctly kept as separate findings by their authors.

**Maturity laundering**: The clearest confirmed instance is internal to the document itself, correctly caught by Report 04 (Finding 4): line 94 and line 583 assert "быстрее = лучше выбирать нужное разрешение" as a flat working characterization; line 813 explicitly downgrades the same claim to "research candidate, а не уже установленный алгоритм learning"; line 819 reopens it as an unresolved tension. That is maturity laundering *inside* the source document across its own timeline of additions, not an auditor artifact — three different confidence levels attached to one claim, in the order settled → hedged → reopened, without the earlier statements being revised. A second, weaker instance: Report 03 flags that "Residual" (line 767-768) is introduced with an honest, correctly-hedged disclaimer ("research candidate," "not schema-independent") but is never invoked again anywhere in the document — this is not committed laundering (the disclaimer has never yet been violated) but a structural vulnerability to *future* laundering, and 03 is careful to phrase it exactly that way rather than overclaiming current damage.

**False precision (cross-report)**: The clearest instance is in Cluster 2. Four reports examine what is essentially the same "materiality is never operationalized" gap, applied to four different sites, and use verdict language ranging from mild ("unresolved — correctly identified as unresolved by the document itself," Report 02) to harsh ("rejected as currently specified," Report 06). Report 06's instance does carry one additional concrete defect the others lack (the "novelty" signal is trivially satisfied by any new candidate, creating a specific loophole), which partly justifies the harsher verdict — but a reader skimming verdict labels alone would come away thinking Report 06 found a worse problem than Reports 02/04/05, when all four are circling the identical underlying gap with different degrees of elaboration. This is worth flagging explicitly as a place where confidence language across reports is not well calibrated to a shared underlying fact pattern.

**Hidden taxonomies in fixes** (violating the anti-reification instruction): The clearest instance is Report 01's Finding 5 fix: "the document doesn't state that some relation-types (causal influence, mechanism) have a restricted status-range (never 'directly observed'...), while others (identity, temporal relation) may legitimately be directly observed." That sentence is drafting the skeleton of a type→allowed-status compatibility table — a small classification scheme — under cover of "just a scope note." It should be flagged and either dropped or rewritten as a single general principle ("some relation-types are, by their nature, never directly observable — only inferred") without naming which specific types get which specific status ranges, which is the part that starts to look like a mapping rule. A secondary, softer case: Report 01's Finding 4 fix ("contested is a cross-cutting property that can co-occur with any of the other six") is technically a two-axis scheme (acquisition-path × dispute-state), but I judge this one acceptable — it corrects an axis-conflation already present in the text rather than inventing new content, which is closer to what the anti-reification instruction is trying to permit than to forbid.

**Implementation drift**: No blatant violations found. The closest candidate is Report 02's Finding 2 treating "BROAD ORIENTATION → CANDIDATE SELECTION → TARGETED REREAD/SOURCE REPLAY → UPDATED MODEL" (line 877) as a literal sequential pipeline step subject to a discovery-lawsuit counterexample — but unlike the top-level map (which carries an explicit "not a mandatory execution order" disclaimer at line 14), this particular retrieval-logic line carries no equivalent disclaimer in its own vicinity, so treating it as a named step with an implied adequacy question is a defensible reading of what's actually on the page, not an invented implementation assumption.

**Circular definitions**: None found. Every fix I checked (06's world-time/belief-time split, 07's SYMMETRIC/SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE proposal, 01's Finding 7 cross-reference fix) defines its target using already-independently-defined terms rather than the term itself or an equally-undefined sibling.

**AI-consensus-as-evidence**: This is worth addressing head-on, since Cluster 1 in particular is exactly the shape of finding that could be mistaken for "true because seven models said so." What actually justifies treating Cluster 1 (and Clusters 2-4) as real is not that four or seven independent audits converged — it's that I went back to the *source document* and confirmed the same structural pattern recurs across genuinely separately-drafted sections of that one document (memory-edge line 113, understanding-edge line 408, semantic-clarification 738-746, person-understanding 692-731, sufficiency-section 792-799 for Cluster 1 alone). The convergence of seven independent readers is what made me *look* for this recurrence — it is a good prioritization signal — but the recurrence itself, verified directly in the text, is what makes the cluster real. A reader should not cite "four auditors agreed" as the evidence; they should cite the five source passages.

**Unsupported universal claims**: Scanning for "never"/"always" language: most instances are either logically entailed by the document's own stated mechanism (03's "reopen structurally cannot address case (c)" is an analytic consequence of how reopen is defined, not an inductive overgeneralization) or already explicitly hedged ("plausibly," "seems," Report 06 Finding 1). I found one instance worth softening: Report 02's Finding 6 claim that the compression test "is never explicitly extended" to live-candidate contraction is stated flatly as "never," which is fair only because it's a claim about the *entire document's* coverage (verifiable by a full read, which I performed and confirms it) — not a claim built from one or two local examples, so it clears the bar the checklist is testing for, but it's worth noting explicitly that this is the kind of "never" claim that needs full-document verification to earn, and it has it here.

**Contradiction between agents**: I did not find a genuine contradiction (two reports reaching incompatible verdicts on the same passage). The closest near-miss — Report 07 praising ROUTINE≠IRRELEVANT's cross-reference to cumulative drift (line 724) as the one part of the person-understanding section that "does inherit an established discipline properly," while Report 04 examines the same lines (715-724) and finds "material deviation" still lacks any operational criterion — turns out not to be a contradiction on inspection: 07 is praising the *existence of the cross-reference*, 04 is critiquing the *absence of a criterion despite the cross-reference*. Both are correct simultaneously; this is complementary precision, not disagreement, and is worth citing as a model of how independently-drafted reports can examine the same three lines without either restating or contradicting the other.
