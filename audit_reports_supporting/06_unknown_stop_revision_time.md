# Adversarial Audit — UNKNOWN / STOP / REVISION / TIME

Framing: This edge of the document is unusually self-aware — it explicitly disclaims closed taxonomies for both UNKNOWN and STOP, and states a genuine pragmatist test ("does the distinction change what's rational to do next") rather than just asserting more guardrail slogans. That is real positive content, not just negative fencing. But the same generosity that keeps UNKNOWN and STOP open-ended also leaves a specific, recurring conceptual gap unaddressed: the document never cleanly separates *why* a belief changed (world moved vs. pure re-derivation of the same evidence vs. genuinely new evidence about an unchanged world), and its one closed-looking artifact in this territory — the six-item STOP-reason list — quietly mixes an epistemic/resource register with a governance register the rest of the document is otherwise careful to keep apart. Below I go deep on both.

---

## Finding 1 — The UNKNOWN "changes what's rational to do next" test is real, but its substrate-neutrality has a scope limit in adversarial/multi-agent settings

**SOURCE:** "Semantic clarification" section: UNKNOWN is "address-specific... relative to SYSTEM × QUESTION × CONTEXT × TIME × AVAILABLE GROUNDS," with a list of subtypes (not searched / not found in this retrieval / source unavailable / evidence doesn't discriminate live alternatives / grounds conflict-contested / question underdetermined by available grounds / claim inapplicable in this scope) explicitly marked "not a closed taxonomy." The stated test: "Главный тест: меняет ли различение то, что рационально делать дальше. Если не меняет, отдельная категория может быть лишней" (the main test is whether the distinction changes what's rational to do next; if it doesn't, the separate category may be superfluous).

**INFERENCE:** This is a legitimate, workable heuristic for a single reasoner introspecting on its own epistemic state — it correctly tells you when to *collapse* categories (e.g. "not searched" and "not found in this retrieval" often cash out to the same next action, "search more/differently," so the test would rightly merge them) as well as when to keep them apart.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (institutional/legal + medical):** In a regulatory or appellate dispute, two parties may disagree about whether a given UNKNOWN is "grounds conflict-contested" (dueling expert testimony) or "question underdetermined by available grounds" (the statute/protocol simply doesn't reach this fact pattern). Under the document's own test, the way to decide whether these deserve separate treatment is to ask whether they imply different rational next actions — but adversarial parties will *also* disagree about what's rational to do next (call another expert vs. seek legislative/precedent clarification), precisely because their institutional interests differ. The test doesn't resolve the category boundary; it relocates the disagreement one level down to "rational for whom." A parallel case in medicine: "biopsy inconclusive" (evidence doesn't discriminate) vs. "second pathologist disagrees with the first" (grounds conflict-contested) — here the test works cleanly (order a more specific stain vs. get a tiebreaker read), because there's a single non-adversarial decision-maker. The test's reliability seems to depend on whether there's one coherent "rational agent" whose next action can be asked about — it degrades exactly in the institutional/legal domain where UNKNOWN classification is itself contested.

**VERDICT:** survives with scope — genuinely load-bearing for a single reasoner or a non-adversarial institutional process; not shown, and plausibly not substrate-neutral, in adversarial/multi-party settings where "what's rational to do next" is itself the disputed question.

## Finding 2a — The divergence-diagnosis list conflates "world changed," "system reassessed with no new observation," and "new evidence about an unchanged world"

**SOURCE:** "При расхождении — сначала локализовать, а не объяснять" lists candidate loci for a mismatch between expectation and observation: "ошибочно само текущее понимание" (current understanding itself is wrong); "ожидание неправильно выведено..." (expectation wrongly derived); "изменилось время или условия" (time or conditions changed); "при прежнем сжатии потеряно важное различие" (an important distinction was lost during earlier compression); "источник неполон..." (source incomplete); "причина остаётся UNKNOWN." This list is explicitly offered as an open, non-exhaustive set of places to look, with "РАСХОЖДЕНИЕ ≠ ОБЪЯСНЕНИЕ" (divergence ≠ explanation) as the governing guardrail.

**INFERENCE:** The list is genuinely rich and avoids the crude "mismatch → world changed" or "mismatch → we were wrong" jumps. But it never poses, as its own first-order question, the triage: *did anything new get observed at all?* "Current understanding itself is wrong" and "an important distinction lost during compression" are both symptoms of type (b) — the system re-examining material it already had, with zero new observation — but the list doesn't name this as a category, and nothing distinguishes it from "time or conditions changed" (type a, the world moving) or "source incomplete" (type c, new evidence trickling in about an unchanged world). A system following this list could legitimately land on "current understanding was wrong" when the real story is "the world changed and our old understanding was correct when formed" (a→wrongly recorded as b), or vice versa.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (formal mathematics — the cleanest case for isolating type b):** A journal retracts a published proof after a colleague, using only the same axioms and lemmas already in the paper, finds a step doesn't follow. No new theorem, no new data, no new "observation" of any kind — pure re-derivation of fully-available material. This is a paradigm case of type (b) with *zero* world component and *zero* new-evidence component. Under the document's list, this would presumably fall under "ошибочно само текущее понимание" — correctly located, but nothing in the document flags that this diagnosis carries a distinct implication from "time or conditions changed": it means the system's own reasoning is unstable under re-derivation of fixed material, which is a reliability defect in the reasoner, not a fact about the world at all. The document has the vocabulary to distinguish these (SOURCE ≠ REPRESENTATION ≠ CLAIM ≠ EVIDENCE ≠ BELIEF ≠ KNOWLEDGE firewall, elsewhere) but never applies it to separate "the world's target state changed" from "we recomputed on the same inputs and got a different answer."

**VERDICT:** partially survives — the underlying epistemic care is present, but the specific three-way distinction the role brief asks about is nowhere made explicit, and the document's own list can be satisfied by evidence that is ambiguous between the three cases without flagging the ambiguity.

## Finding 2b — "TIME" and "currentness" are bundled as one field everywhere they appear; world-time and system/record-time are never split into separate axes

**SOURCE:** Meaning Envelope: "TIME / currentness" listed as a single item (line ~873); memory-provenance section: "source identity/version, provenance, контекст, time/currentness, статус утверждения..." (line ~869); thinking-checks list: "ВРЕМЯ — старое знание всё ещё применимо к текущему состоянию?" (does old knowledge still apply to the current state?) (line ~178). The UNKNOWN address itself uses a single "TIME" axis in "SYSTEM × QUESTION × CONTEXT × TIME × AVAILABLE GROUNDS."

**INFERENCE:** In every place the document names a "time" dimension, it is grammatically fused with "currentness"/applicability rather than split into (i) *when the state of affairs held in the world* and (ii) *when the system came to represent/believe it*. This matters because "is old knowledge still applicable now?" (line 178) can fail for entirely different reasons depending on which axis moved, and the fix differs completely.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (medical, changing-world domain):** A 2024 pathology report says "no metastasis," accurately, at the time. In 2026 someone asks whether it's "still applicable." Three distinct scenarios: (1) the cancer progressed since 2024 — world-time changed, the old report was correct-then and is simply superseded now; the right move is to re-scan the *current* patient. (2) On relabeling slides, staff discover the original slide was misread due to poor staining — pure reassessment, same physical slide, no new observation, world never changed; the right move is to correct the *historical* record and audit any decisions made downstream of the wrong belief. (3) The original report was accurate and the world hasn't changed, but a newly available, more sensitive marker test independently corroborates it — new evidence about an unchanged world; the right move is simply to raise confidence, touching neither the patient's current status nor the old record. The document's single fused "time/currentness" field cannot represent the difference between these three, yet a system that treats them identically will, in scenario (2), literally start re-scanning a patient instead of correcting an error, or in scenario (1), "correct the record" for a fact that was never wrong.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE 2 (user interaction/performative feedback — same conflation recurs independently):** The personalization section states "PAST USER PATTERN ≠ CURRENT USER IDENTITY" and requires the user model to stay revisable, but doesn't distinguish: the person's actual preferences changed (world/person-time moved); the system misread old interaction logs and is only now correcting its own reading (pure reassessment, person unchanged); or the user just explicitly stated a preference that was true all along but never previously surfaced (new evidence, person unchanged). That the same three-way ambiguity resurfaces in an independently-drafted section (personalization) rather than only in the memory/provenance section suggests this is a structural gap in the underlying model, not a local phrasing accident.

**VERDICT:** rejected as currently written — the document has all the adjacent vocabulary (CURRENT MODEL ≠ SOURCE REALITY, REVISION ≠ HISTORY DISAPPEARED) to build this distinction, but nowhere states world-time and system/belief-time as two separate axes; "time/currentness" is treated as one dimension throughout, and the ambiguity recurs across at least two independently-written sections. This is not a new-primitive problem — it is a relation that needs to be split: **"when the described state of affairs held" is a different axis from "when the system's record of it was formed/last revisited,"** and the document should say so explicitly wherever it currently writes "time/currentness" as a single term.

## Finding 3 — The Self-Effect Discrimination A–D taxonomy structurally cannot cover the "zero new observation" revision case, though it is the document's nearest built-out machinery for exactly this kind of question

**SOURCE:** Self-Effect Discrimination: "При новом наблюдении полезно сохранять возможность различить хотя бы: A. мир изменился независимо от системы; B. наблюдаемое стало следствием собственного предыдущего действия; C. сигнал создан или искажён самим способом измерения; D. причина смешанная или пока UNKNOWN." ("At a new observation, it's useful to be able to distinguish at least: A. the world changed independently of the system; B. the observed thing is a consequence of the system's own prior action; C. the signal was created/distorted by the measurement itself; D. mixed or UNKNOWN.")

**INFERENCE:** This is the one place in the document that names "world changed" as an explicit, first-class category (A) — exactly the kind of distinction the role brief is looking for. But the entire frame is triggered by "При новом наблюдении" (at a new observation): it presupposes something was newly observed and asks what caused *that observation*. It has no branch for the case where revision happens with no new observation at all (pure re-derivation/reinterpretation of already-fully-available evidence — Finding 2a's type b). A reader could easily assume this A–D list is the document's general answer to "what changed," when it actually only answers a narrower question (attribution of a *new* observed change) that structurally excludes the no-new-observation case.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (constitutive rules / protocol domain):** Consider a chess engine's evaluation of a stored position changing from a previous session, with no new game played and no new position observed — the engine simply re-runs its own search with more depth on the identical, already-fully-available board state and reaches a different evaluation. There is no "new observation" here in any meaningful sense (the board state was already fully known), so A/B/C/D (world-changed / self-caused / measurement artifact / mixed) don't apply at all — none of the four categories fit, because all four assume an observation exists to attribute. The document offers no fifth bucket for "revised our reading of already-complete information."

**VERDICT:** partially survives — the A–D taxonomy is well-scoped and useful *for what it actually claims to cover*; the gap is that nothing in the document flags that it does not extend to the zero-new-observation case, so a reader could mistake it for a general-purpose world-vs-belief discriminator when it is not one. No new Engine needed — the gap is simply that the document should note explicitly that self-effect discrimination and pure re-derivation are different failure surfaces, not silently let the more developed one stand in for the underdeveloped one.

## Finding 4 — Cumulative drift (100→99.9→99.8→...) is ambiguous among world-drift, belief-drift, and evidence-drift, and its one proposed detection mechanism is explicitly unproven

**SOURCE:** "ЛОКАЛЬНО НЕЗНАЧИТЕЛЬНОЕ ≠ ГЛОБАЛЬНО НЕЗНАЧИТЕЛЬНОЕ" with the 100→99.9→99.8→... sequence; "Рабочий кандидат для дальнейшего исследования: возможно, повторная диагностика старого понимания и сравнение ответов со старыми ответами позволяет увидеть дрейф. Это пока гипотеза, а не установленный механизм." (working candidate: possibly re-diagnosing old understanding and comparing answers to old answers reveals drift — this is a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.)

**INFERENCE:** The metaphor itself is neutral among (a) the world's target state is slowly, genuinely changing across the sequence (e.g., gradual disease progression); (b) the system is re-assessing the *same* fixed case repeatedly and its own answer is drifting with no new observations at all (an instability/reliability problem in the reasoner, not in the world); (c) small pieces of new evidence are trickling in incrementally about an unchanged underlying fact (ordinary Bayesian updating, healthy). The remedy differs totally across the three: (a) calls for more frequent re-observation of the world; (b) calls for auditing the reasoner's own stability under repeated reflection on fixed inputs; (c) calls for nothing beyond checking the update arithmetic. The document's own proposed detection candidate — "re-diagnose old understanding and compare to old answers" — is itself most naturally suited to detecting case (b) (it holds the input fixed and looks for drifting output), yet the document frames it as a general drift-detector without flagging that it wouldn't even fire on case (a) or (c), where the *input itself* is legitimately different each time.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (medical, changing-world domain):** A diagnostic confidence score drifts 100%→99%→98%→...→90% over successive visits. If this reflects the patient's condition genuinely worsening (world-drift), the clinically correct response is escalated monitoring — the *number* dropping is appropriate and should not be "corrected." If it instead reflects the same static chart being re-read slightly differently by rotating on-call physicians (system-drift, no new observation), the correct response is to flag inter-rater instability in the interpretation process itself — a completely different intervention. Conflating the two risks either alarming clinicians over normal disease progression, or missing a real interpretive-drift problem in the diagnostic process because it "looks like" ordinary disease trajectory.

**VERDICT:** unresolved — explicitly and honestly flagged by the document itself as "a hypothesis, not an established mechanism," which is appropriate epistemic humility; the substantive gap is that even as a hypothesis, the proposed mechanism is not shown to discriminate world-drift from belief-drift from evidence-drift, and the document doesn't note this limitation on its own candidate.

## Finding 5 — The six STOP-reasons mix an epistemic/resource register with a governance/authority register, which is exactly the kind of category-merge the document elsewhere refuses to allow

**SOURCE:** "Остановка может означать достаточность для текущего вопроса, отсутствие различающего evidence, предел ресурсов, недоступный источник, внешний interruption или границу authority." (Stopping can mean: sufficiency for the current question, absence of discriminating evidence, resource limits, unavailable source, external interruption, or an authority boundary.) Explicitly not a closed enum. Compare firewall §24: "COGNITIVE REPRESENTATION DOES NOT GENERATE AUTHORITY... KNOWLEDGE ≠ RECOMMENDATION ≠ DECISION ≠ AUTHORIZATION ≠ ACTION" and §20's "CAPABILITY ≠ PERMISSION."

**INFERENCE:** Five of the six listed stop-reasons (sufficiency, absent discriminating evidence, resource limits, unavailable source, external interruption) are epistemic or resource-cost facts about the inquiry itself. The sixth — "authority boundary" — is categorically different: it is a *permission* fact about who is allowed to keep looking, not a fact about whether more looking would be informative. The document is elsewhere extremely careful to keep "what the system knows/can conclude" separate from "what the system is permitted to do" (the entire COGNITION→AGENCY section, and the CAPABILITY≠PERMISSION firewall). By listing "authority boundary" as one item in the same STOP-reason enumeration as five purely epistemic reasons, the document invites exactly the conflation it guards against elsewhere: a reader could treat "we stopped because of an authority boundary" as informationally equivalent to "we stopped because the evidence was sufficient" — both just "STOP," differing only in which of six interchangeable flavors applies — when in fact an authority-boundary stop tells you *nothing* about the epistemic state of the question at all, and reopening it requires an entirely different act (obtaining permission) than reopening any of the other five (obtaining or re-deriving evidence).

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (institutional/legal, constitutive-rules domain):** A regulatory investigation is closed because the statute of limitations ran out (authority boundary) versus a second, unrelated investigation closed because the evidence collected was sufficient to clear the subject (sufficiency). Both show up externally as "case closed." But the first tells a future investigator nothing about whether wrongdoing occurred — new evidence could emerge that would have been damning, yet reopening is blocked by law regardless of evidential materiality — while the second is a genuine epistemic stop that should reopen if a genuinely new, discriminating fact surfaces. Filing both under one "STOP" umbrella with only "SAME OUTWARD STOP ≠ SAME MATERIAL STOP BASIS" (§24) as the safeguard puts the entire weight of distinguishing them on a reader who has to already know to ask, since the document's own STOP-reason list doesn't flag that one of its six items lives in a different logical register than the other five.

**VERDICT:** partially survives — "SAME STOP ≠ SAME EPISTEMIC STATE" is the right instinct and the guardrail sentence is true, but the document's own worked example of stop-reasons undermines its own discipline by putting a governance-boundary reason in the same list as five epistemic-boundary reasons without flagging the register difference. This is not a call for a new StopReason-enum implementation (explicitly against the anti-reification constraint) — it is a request to separate two already-distinct relations the document uses elsewhere: *epistemic sufficiency* and *authorization boundary* should not share one undifferentiated list entry structure, even in prose.

## Finding 6 — Reopen is gated entirely by "materiality," which is never grounded at the point of use; stress-tested in both directions the gap is real but asymmetric

**SOURCE:** STOP section: "...сохранять возможность понять, почему inquiry остановился и что могло бы его reopen" — no criterion given beyond preserving the *possibility* of understanding this. SLED/SUT section: "СЛЕД = достаточный путь назад к основанию там, где такой пересмотр materially важен" — reopen is gated on "materially important" with no further definition at that point. Elsewhere, §6 (legacy layer) *does* unpack materiality generically: "Candidate materiality signals: влияние на текущий вывод; противоречие; высокая causal/dependency leverage; граница применимости; необратимость; novelty/anomaly; uncertainty; изменение trajectory; evidence gap; возможность изменить ответ или действие" — but this list is never cross-referenced from the STOP/reopen passages.

**INFERENCE (too loose direction):** If the §6 materiality-signal list were used as a *sufficient* (rather than jointly-weighed) test for whether a newly surfaced candidate should reopen a stop, note that "novelty/anomaly" is on the list — and a "newly surfaced candidate" is, definitionally, novel. Any new candidate trivially satisfies at least this one materiality signal, so a naive reading of the existing list, applied without a weighing/thresholding step the document never supplies, does risk the failure mode the role brief names: any new candidate → automatic reopen, defeating STOP as a resource/authority-respecting boundary.

**INFERENCE (too strict direction):** Conversely, the STOP passage only obligates *preserving the capability* to understand what could reopen an inquiry ("сохранять возможность понять... что могло бы его reopen") — it does not obligate any active check for whether that reopening condition has actually been met. "We could, in principle, tell you why we stopped and what would change our mind" is a much weaker commitment than "we periodically or reactively check whether that condition now holds." Nothing in the document creates a duty to re-examine a stop; only a duty to be *able to explain* it if asked. Read strictly, this leans toward STOP being effectively permanent by default — reopening only happens if some outside process happens to ask the right question, not because the system monitors for its own reopen conditions.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE (resource-bounded inquiry):** An engineering team stops investigating an intermittent production bug after a week, citing resource limits, having concluded "probably a rare race condition, low priority." Two competing failure modes: (i) if every subsequent bug report that even superficially resembles the old one counts as "novel" and therefore material, the team is obligated to reopen the investigation weekly, forever, and the STOP achieves nothing — this is the too-loose failure. (ii) if the team's only obligation was ever to be *able to say* why they stopped (resource limits, low estimated impact) and never to actively re-check that estimate, then even a genuinely new, cheap, highly diagnostic candidate (a colleague notices the failures cluster exactly around a specific deploy) sits unexamined indefinitely, because nothing in the process requires anyone to check it against the old stop's basis — this is the too-strict failure. The document's guardrail language ("preserve the possibility of understanding what could reopen it") is compatible with both failure modes and adjudicates neither.

**VERDICT:** rejected as currently specified, though NOT because a reopen-trigger algorithm is missing — per the task constraint, that gap should be reported, not filled. The concrete, fixable problem is narrower than "invent reopen logic": the document already has a materiality-signal list (§6) that is a plausible candidate vocabulary for reopen-worthiness, but (1) it is never bound to the STOP/reopen passages by cross-reference, leaving the reader to guess whether it applies there at all, and (2) even where it would apply, the document never states whether these signals are meant to be individually sufficient or need joint/weighted satisfaction — a distinction that matters enormously given that "novelty" alone is satisfied by definition by anything newly proposed. **No new primitive required** — the fix is to state the relation between the existing §6 materiality-signal list and the STOP/reopen passages, and to say whether a single signal suffices or several must jointly hold; that is a relation-binding problem, not an algorithm-design problem.

## Closing: what held up vs. what's genuinely broken

**Held up / not a real issue:**
- The refusal to give UNKNOWN or STOP a closed enum is a deliberate and correct choice, not vagueness for its own sake — the document is explicit about this ("не закрытая taxonomy," "этот блок не вводит закрытый enum причин STOP") and the pragmatist "does it change what's rational to do next" test (Finding 1) is a real, usable criterion in single-reasoner and non-adversarial-institutional settings, which covers most of the document's own use cases.
- "Revision does not erase history" (CURRENT UNDERSTANDING CHANGED ≠ HISTORY DISAPPEARED) is internally consistent and well-formed on its own terms; the six revision-types listed (clarify, narrow/widen, split regime, drop relation, keep competing explanations, move to UNKNOWN) are a genuinely useful, non-trivial taxonomy of *what a revision can look like* — my finding is only that this list is silent on *what triggered* the revision (world/belief/evidence), not that the list itself is wrong or empty.
- The Self-Effect Discrimination A–D taxonomy (Finding 3) is well-scoped and correctly humble ("проблема, а не биологическая схема её решения") for the narrower question it actually answers; the issue is scope-creep risk for a reader, not internal incoherence.

**Genuinely broken / unresolved:**
- World-time vs. system/belief-time are never separated as distinct axes anywhere "time" or "currentness" is used (Finding 2b) — this is the sharpest, most load-bearing gap on this edge, and it recurs independently in at least two sections (memory/provenance and personalization), which argues against it being a one-off phrasing slip.
- The three-way conflation of world-changed / pure-reassessment / new-evidence-about-unchanged-world (Finding 2a) is real and is not resolved by any existing machinery in the document, including the nearest-adjacent Self-Effect Discrimination apparatus (Finding 3), which structurally can't reach the zero-new-observation case.
- The STOP-reason list's silent mixing of an epistemic/resource register with an authority/governance register (Finding 5) is a genuine internal tension against the document's own COGNITION→AGENCY firewall discipline.
- Reopen criteria remain ungrounded at their point of use, with a plausible but unbound fix already sitting elsewhere in the document (Finding 6) — this is a missing cross-reference and a missing joint/sufficient distinction, not a missing algorithm, and should be reported and fixed as exactly that.
